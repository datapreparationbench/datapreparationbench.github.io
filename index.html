TL;DR

DataPrep-Bench is the first unified, downstream-grounded benchmark that jointly evaluates how well LLMs, agents, and data workflows can prepare training data end to end. It covers two complementary tracks — Data Construction (transforming raw sources into SFT data) and Data Quality Evaluation (predicting downstream utility of candidate datasets) — and ships two strong baselines: Data-Construction-Skill (skill-driven agentic construction) and the Distributional Alignment Score (DAS) (training-free, MMD-based quality estimator).

01Abstract

The quality of training data fundamentally determines the capabilities of large language models (LLMs). As the community increasingly relies on LLMs, agents, and data-centric workflows to produce and curate training corpora, a foundational question emerges: how well can these systems actually prepare training data end to end? Despite the rapid proliferation of LLM-driven data preparation techniques, no unified benchmark exists to systematically measure their effectiveness.

We view LLM-driven data preparation as comprising two complementary capabilities: data construction, which transforms raw sources into high-quality training data, and data quality evaluation, which predicts the training value of candidate datasets before downstream training. We introduce DataPrep-Bench, the first comprehensive benchmark that jointly evaluates both capabilities as first-class targets under a unified, downstream-grounded protocol.

DataPrep-Bench is organized around two tracks. (1) Data Construction takes low-quality or otherwise non-trainable sources (e.g., domain books) as input and asks agents or workflows to transform them into supervised training data; the produced data is evaluated end to end by the downstream performance of models fine-tuned on it. We release Data-Construction-Skill as a strong baseline. (2) Data Quality Evaluation takes mainstream candidate training datasets and produces scalar scores; its goal is to measure whether a scoring function is linearly predictive of downstream utility. We release the Distributional Alignment Score (DAS) as a strong baseline. Across multiple domains and architectures, DAS achieves strong overall correlation with downstream performance and outperforms existing quality-, diversity-, and heuristic-based evaluators in most settings.

02Framework Overview

DataPrep-Bench formalizes data preparation under a unified, downstream-grounded framework. Both tracks share the same evaluation principle: a data preparation decision is useful only if it leads to better downstream model performance. Concretely, the common reference signal is \( \mathrm{Perf}\bigl(f(\mathcal{X}), \mathcal{T}_k\bigr) \) — the benchmark score of a base model fine-tuned on dataset \(\mathcal{X}\) for domain \(D_k\).

03Two Evaluation Tracks

Track 1

Data Construction

Can LLMs, agents, and workflows transform raw domain sources (books, manuals, long-form materials) into useful supervised fine-tuning data?

\[ \mathrm{Score}_{\mathrm{con}}(M, D_k) \;=\; \mathrm{Perf}\!\left( f\!\left(\mathcal{X}_k^{(M)}\right),\; \mathcal{T}_k \right) \]
  • Input. Curated source corpus \( \mathcal{B}_k = \{b_{k,1}, \dots, b_{k,B_k}\} \) — domain books and long-form knowledge in unified Markdown.
  • Output. Synthesized SFT dataset \( \mathcal{X}_k^{(M)} = M(\mathcal{B}_k) = \{(q_i, a_i)\} \).
  • Evaluation. Fine-tune base model on \( \mathcal{X}_k^{(M)} \), measure performance on held-out benchmarks \(\mathcal{T}_k\).
  • Strong baseline: Data-Construction-Skill — skill-guided agentic construction.
Track 2

Data Quality Evaluation

Can a scoring function \(Q\) predict downstream utility of candidate training datasets before fine-tuning?

\[ \rho(Q, D_k) = \mathrm{Pearson}\!\left( \mathbf{q}_k,\; \mathbf{s}_k \right),\;\; \mathbf{s}_k = \{\mathrm{Perf}(f(\mathcal{X}_{k,i}), \mathcal{T}_k)\} \]
  • Input. Candidate dataset pool \( \mathbb{X}_k = \{\mathcal{X}_{k,1}, \dots, \mathcal{X}_{k,C_k}\} \) (in-domain & out-of-domain), plus optional domain proxy.
  • Output. Per-dataset scalar score \( q_{k,i} = Q(\mathcal{X}_{k,i}) \).
  • Evaluation. Pearson \( \rho \) between \(\mathbf{q}_k\) and empirical fine-tuned performance \(\mathbf{s}_k\), with significance \(p < 0.05\).
  • Strong baseline: Distributional Alignment Score (DAS) — MMD-based, training-free.

04Strong Baselines

For Track 1 · Data Construction

Data-Construction-Skill

A skill-guided agentic framework that turns long-form domain books into reusable SFT supervision. The novelty is treating skills — explicit specifications of sample types, output schemas, filtering rules and validation — as a reusable control layer that sits between high-level construction goals and low-level agent execution.

1
Structure-Aware Chunking
Partition the corpus \( \mathcal{B}_k \) into semantically coherent chunks \( \mathcal{C}_k = \mathrm{Chunk}(\mathcal{B}_k) \), preserving local completeness while keeping inputs manageable.
2
Skill-Regulated Agent
A reusable skill specifies sample types, output schema, filtering criteria, coverage requirements, and validation behaviors. The agent plans over the corpus and invokes utilities under skill-level constraints.
3
Three-Form QA Generation
For each retained chunk \(c\): \( \mathcal{G}(c) = \mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{concept}}(c) \cup \mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{reason}}(c) \cup \mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{case}}(c) \) — concept, reasoning and case-based QA.
4
Post-Processing & Validation
Strip document-relative phrasing, filter malformed/duplicated/weakly-grounded samples, maintain chunk-level processing records for coverage and resumability.
The skill instructs the agent to produce QA pairs that are faithful to the source, self-contained (no document-relative phrasing), diverse in question type and difficulty, and useful as SFT examples — while explicitly listing invalid patterns (passage-dependent prompts, hallucinated answers, duplicates).
For Track 2 · Data Quality Evaluation

Distributional Alignment Score (DAS)

A training-free, distribution-level data quality metric grounded in domain-adaptation theory. DAS instantiates \(Q\) as the (negative) MMD between a candidate dataset and a domain proxy.

Score Definition
\[ \mathrm{DAS}(\mathcal{X}_{k,i}) \;=\; -\, d_{\mathrm{MMD}}\!\left( \Phi(\mathcal{X}_{k,i}),\; \Phi(\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{proxy}}_k);\; \kappa \right) \]
1
Encode
Map each text sample with a fixed encoder \(\phi\) (Qwen3-Embedding-8B, 4096-dim). Both candidate and domain proxy are encoded identically for comparability.
2
Measure
Empirical MMD with a Gaussian RBF kernel between candidate and proxy feature distributions. Sample 5,000 instances per dataset; bandwidth \(\sigma=1.0\).
3
Score
Higher DAS ⟹ stronger distributional alignment with the domain proxy ⟹ higher predicted downstream utility.
Motivated by the domain-adaptation bound (Theorem 36, Redko et al., 2020): under a fixed model family and training protocol, a smaller \(d_{\mathrm{MMD}}\) yields a tighter upper bound on target risk. The triangle inequality further justifies a proxy-based formulation, avoiding benchmark contamination.

05Benchmarks & Experimental Setup

Downstream-grounded evaluation
Both tracks are evaluated by downstream performance under a shared protocol. Track 1 measures the utility of constructed data through SFT; Track 2 evaluates whether a metric predicts downstream utility via Pearson correlation with significance.
Domains & target benchmarks
  • General: MMLU-Redux
  • Math: AIME24, AMC23, Gaokao2024, GSM8K, MATH, MinervaMath, OlympiadBench
  • Science: MMLU-STEM, MMLU-Pro, GPQA, SuperGPQA, ChemBench, PIQA, SciBench
  • Medical: MedR-Bench, MedMCQA, MedCaseReasoning
  • Finance: XFinBench, FinEval-KR, CPA-QKA
  • Law: LegalBench, LexGLUE
Base models
  • Track 1: Qwen2.5-7B, Llama-3.1-8B
  • Track 2: Qwen2.5-7B, Llama-3.1-8B, Mistral-7B-v0.3
Track 2 spans three architectural families to test whether observed correlations are model-dependent.
Fine-tuning protocol
All models fine-tuned via LlamaFactory under a consistent recipe: 3 epochs, cosine LR, initial LR \(5.0 \times 10^{-6}\), warmup ratio 0.1, per-device batch 1, gradient accumulation 4 (global batch 32), 8×H20 cluster. Non-instruct base models are augmented with Dolly-15k.
Source corpus (Track 1)
Domain books from Wikibooks / FreeBookCentre / Open Textbook Library; Chinese university and competition materials for Math/Science; FineWeb 150 MB sample (100 Markdown files) for General. All PDFs converted to Markdown via MinerU for a unified input format.
Candidate pools (Track 2)
For each domain, an in-domain pool (e.g., DataFlow-10k, WizardLM, UltraChat, OpenR1-Math-220k, MegaScience, UltraMedical, …) plus an out-of-domain pool drawn from other domains. Each candidate is fine-tuned and benchmarked to provide ground-truth downstream utility.

08Key Findings

Constructed data must be evaluated end-to-end

SFT on synthesized data does not always improve downstream performance. Outcomes are strongly domain- and model-dependent — motivating downstream-grounded evaluation rather than surface-level quality proxies.

Direct LLM & fixed workflows: useful but limited

Direct LLM generation provides a simple baseline but is unstable; DataFlow-based workflows excel where corpora can be transformed via stable procedures (best Qwen Law 77.2; best Llama Finance 36.5) but are weaker on Math and Science.

Agent-based construction shows strong potential

Agents frequently take top spots in Math, Finance, Medicine, Law — e.g., agent GPT-5.2 25.5 (Qwen Math), agent GPT-5.3-codex 59.3 (Qwen Finance), agent Gemini 3.0 Pro 43.8 (Qwen Medicine).

Skill-based construction is a strong baseline

Data-Construction-Skill ties for best on Qwen General (78.2) and best on Minerva-Math (14.0); on Llama-3.1-8B Finance it lifts no-synthetic from 15.1 to 34.2. Not yet uniformly dominant — leaving headroom for future skills.

DAS strongly predicts downstream utility

DAS exhibits consistent, statistically significant correlations with empirical performance: Math +0.86 Avg (Mistral +0.94, Llama +0.93), Medical +0.77, Science +0.72, General Text +0.68.

Cross-domain generalization remains open

No single quality metric is uniformly reliable across all domains. DAS attenuates in Finance (+0.18) and flips direction in Law (−0.36); Superfiltering wins Finance (+0.67) and MTLD wins Law (+0.52) — among the diversity- and quality-based baselines.

DAS is practical at scale

DAS averages 306 s per dataset — substantially faster than Vendi Score (459 s) and Deita-Quality (435 s) — while delivering markedly stronger predictive correlation. A favorable accuracy/efficiency trade-off.

Model-architecture coverage matters

Qwen2.5-7B, Llama-3.1-8B, and Mistral-7B-v0.3 give distinct downstream signals. Track 2 spans all three to test whether observed correlations are model-dependent — DAS remains consistent across architectures in reasoning-intensive domains.

A common testbed for the community

By fixing source corpora, base models, training recipes, and downstream benchmarks, DataPrep-Bench enables head-to-head comparison of agents, workflows, and metrics — turning data preparation into a measurable, reproducible research target.

What you can plug in

Track 1
Bring your own Construction method
Your method takes our Markdown source corpus \(\mathcal{B}_k\) per domain and outputs an SFT dataset. We fine-tune the same base models with the same protocol and report downstream scores.
Track 2
Bring your own Quality metric
Your metric \(Q\) consumes a candidate dataset and returns a scalar. We report Pearson \(\rho\) between \(\{Q(\mathcal{X}_{k,i})\}\) and the released \(\{s_{k,i}\}\) ground-truth downstream scores.
