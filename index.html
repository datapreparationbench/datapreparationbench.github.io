Motivation

Why Benchmark LLMs as Data Preparators?

Training data is a primary driver of LLM capability. As high-quality natural corpora become increasingly scarce, the community is turning to LLMs, agents, and data-centric workflows to produce and curate training data. This raises a foundational question: how well can these systems actually prepare training data end to end?

We study this emerging paradigm as LLM-driven data preparation, which consists of two capabilities: data construction, which transforms raw or otherwise non-trainable sources into supervised training data, and data quality evaluation, which predicts the training value of candidate datasets before downstream fine-tuning.

Existing work has demonstrated that LLM-driven preparation is feasible, but evaluations are often isolated: different studies use different sources, base models, training settings, and metrics. DataPrep-Bench closes this gap with a unified testbed where agents, workflows, and metrics are compared under shared resources and a common downstream-grounded protocol.

Core Question

Rather than asking whether generated data merely appears fluent, diverse, or clean, DataPrep-Bench asks whether the prepared data leads to a better model after fine-tuning, and whether a metric can predict that improvement before training is performed.

Benchmark

DataPrep-Bench

DataPrep-Bench is a comprehensive benchmark for LLM-driven training data preparation. It jointly evaluates Data Construction and Data Quality Evaluation as first-class targets. Both tracks use downstream model performance as the reference signal, enabling principled comparison of agents, workflows, and data quality metrics.

Data Construction Track

Given raw domain sources such as books, technical manuals, or long-form knowledge materials, a construction method must produce useful QA-style supervised fine-tuning data. The output is evaluated by fine-tuning base models on the constructed dataset and measuring held-out domain benchmark performance.

  • Input: raw Markdown-formatted domain sources.
  • Output: supervised QA-style training data.
  • Evaluation: downstream performance induced by the synthesized dataset.

Data Quality Evaluation Track

Given candidate training datasets, a scoring function assigns scalar quality scores before fine-tuning. The metric is evaluated by whether its scores are linearly predictive of the empirical downstream utility of models trained on the corresponding datasets.

  • Input: candidate dataset pools and optional domain proxies.
  • Output: scalar utility scores for candidate datasets.
  • Evaluation: Pearson correlation with downstream performance.

Protocol

Downstream-Grounded Evaluation

DataPrep-Bench evaluates data preparation by its actual training utility. For each candidate supervised fine-tuning dataset, a base model is fine-tuned under a shared training protocol and then evaluated on held-out benchmarks from the corresponding domain. This design avoids relying on surface-level proxies such as fluency, perplexity, or diversity alone.

In the Data Construction Track, the benchmark asks whether a construction method can transform raw domain materials into training data that improves a downstream model. In the Data Quality Evaluation Track, the benchmark asks whether a scoring function can predict which candidate datasets will be useful before the cost of fine-tuning is incurred.

Both tracks are grounded in the same empirical reference signal: the performance of models trained on the prepared or selected data. This enables a unified comparison among LLM generators, agentic workflows, skill-guided systems, and training-free quality estimators.

Step 1

Prepare or Score Data

Construction methods synthesize SFT data from raw sources, while quality metrics assign utility scores to candidate datasets before training.

Step 2

Train Under a Shared Protocol

Base models are fine-tuned using consistent hyperparameters, allowing the benchmark to isolate the effect of data preparation decisions.

Step 3

Measure Downstream Utility

Constructed datasets are evaluated by induced benchmark performance, and quality metrics are evaluated by their correlation with empirical downstream utility.

Methods

Strong Baselines Released with DataPrep-Bench

We release two methodological baselines aligned with the two benchmark tracks: Data-Construction-Skill for skill-guided agentic data construction, and Distributional Alignment Score for training-free data quality evaluation.

Data-Construction-Skill

Data-Construction-Skill introduces skills as a reusable control layer for long-horizon agentic data construction. A skill packages task instructions, output schemas, sample types, filtering rules, coverage requirements, and validation behavior, while the agent remains responsible for planning over the corpus and processing source chunks iteratively.

Concept QA Reasoning QA Case-based QA

The method filters navigational, noisy, duplicated, weakly grounded, and passage-dependent samples, so that the final dataset reflects reusable domain supervision rather than book-comprehension behavior.

Distributional Alignment Score

DAS estimates whether a candidate dataset is distributionally aligned with a domain proxy. Text samples are embedded with a fixed encoder, and Maximum Mean Discrepancy is computed between candidate and proxy feature distributions using a Gaussian RBF kernel.

Since smaller MMD indicates stronger alignment, DAS is defined as negative MMD. Higher DAS therefore predicts higher downstream utility without requiring downstream fine-tuning.

Results

Experimental Results

Experiments across six domains show that data preparation methods can lead to substantially different downstream outcomes. Constructed data does not always improve model performance, highlighting why data preparation must be evaluated under shared source corpora, base models, and benchmarks.

Data-Construction-Skill provides a strong baseline for skill-guided agentic data construction. DAS achieves strong overall correlation with downstream performance across several domains and model architectures, while Finance and Law reveal that no single quality metric is uniformly reliable across all settings.

Main Observations

Key empirical findings from both benchmark tracks.

Evaluation must be end-to-end

Constructed data can improve, plateau, or degrade performance depending on the domain, base model, and alignment of the synthesized data.

Agentic construction is promising

Agents can decompose corpus-level data preparation into reading, extraction, filtering, reformulation, and verification steps.

No metric is universal

DAS strongly predicts utility in several domains, while Finance and Law reveal the limits of a universal data quality metric.
